# Whose avatar was Lakshman?

Lakshmanji is the avatar of Shesh, the thousand-headed serpent who supports the world, according to the Ramcharitmanas of Goswami Tulsidas. The Valmiki Ramayana of Maharshi Valmiki describes Lakshmanji as a portion of Lord Vishnu. These passages express his divine identity differently, while the Ramcharitmanas explicitly names him Shesh.

## The answer in the Ramcharitmanas

Goswami Tulsidas identifies Lakshmanji as Shesh in his opening salutations. He also explains the purpose of this descent: to remove the earth’s fear.

> सेष सहस्रसीस जग कारन। जो अवतरेउ भूमि भय टारन॥
> सदा सो सानुकूल रह मो पर। कृपासिंधु सौमित्रि गुनाकर॥४॥
> Ramcharitmanas, Bal Kand, Doha 17, Chaupai 4

“May Lakshmanji, the son of Sumitra, remain gracious to me. He is the thousand-headed Shesh who supports the world and descended to remove the earth’s fear. He is an ocean of compassion and a storehouse of virtues.”

The word *Saumitri* means the son of Sumitra. Goswami Tulsidas places this affectionate human relationship beside Lakshmanji’s divine identity. The one who bears the world appears as Sumitra’s son.

The same teaching enters the account of his naming:

> लच्छन धाम राम प्रिय सकल जगत आधार।
> गुरु बसिष्ट तेहि राखा लछिमन नाम उदार॥१९७॥
> Ramcharitmanas, Bal Kand, Doha 197

“Guru Vashishth gave him the noble name Lakshman. He is the abode of auspicious qualities, dear to Shri Ram and the support of the whole world.”

In Ayodhya Kand, Maharshi Valmiki likewise tells Shri Ram that Lakshmanji is the thousand-headed lord of serpents who bears the earth. In Lanka Kand, when countless warriors like Meghnad cannot lift Lakshmanji, Goswami Tulsidas again calls him Shesh, the support of the world. Thus, the identification with Shesh is stated clearly in more than one place.

## The answer in the Valmiki Ramayana

The Valmiki Ramayana describes the birth of Lakshmanji and Shatrughna through their relationship with Lord Vishnu:

> अथ लक्ष्मणशत्रुघ्नौ सुमित्राजनयत् सुतौ।
> वीरौ सर्वास्त्रकुशलौ विष्णोरर्धसमन्वितौ॥ १४॥
> Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 18, Shloka 14

“Sumitra then gave birth to two sons, Lakshman and Shatrughna. Both were brave, skilled in every kind of weapon, and endowed with a portion of Lord Vishnu.”

At Lakshmanji’s return to heaven, this divine relationship is stated again:

> ततो विष्णोश्चतुर्भागमागतं सुरसत्तमाः।
> हृष्टाः प्रमुदिताः सर्वे पूजयन्ति स्म राघवम्॥ १८॥
> Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda, Sarga 106, Shloka 18

“The great gods rejoiced when Lakshmanji, the fourth portion of Lord Vishnu, returned to heaven. Filled with gladness, they honoured him.”

The distinction should remain clear. These Valmiki Ramayana passages present Lakshmanji as a portion of Lord Vishnu and do not name him Shesh. The Ramcharitmanas identifies him explicitly as Shesh.

Here, one may wonder how the two descriptions fit together. Many devotees reconcile them by remembering Shesh as the eternal companion of Lord Vishnu. That understanding comes from the wider devotional tradition, not from these verses. We can hold this understanding while allowing each scripture’s particular words to remain clear.

## What Lakshmanji’s identity teaches us

**Strength finds its purpose in service.** Goswami Tulsidas describes the support of the universe as one who descends to remove fear. His power is joined with compassion. The prayer therefore asks for his grace, directing our attention toward the good that strength can accomplish.

For example, a student who understands a difficult lesson can use that ability to help a classmate. The student must listen patiently and explain the steps clearly. Knowing the answer becomes an opportunity to serve. Similarly, reflecting on Lakshmanji’s strength can encourage us to use our abilities to ease another person’s difficulty.

**Divine greatness and affection belong together.** Guru Vashishth’s naming places “dear to Shri Ram” beside “support of the whole world.” We should dwell on both descriptions. The verse gives us divine majesty together with a relationship of love.

Begin with this practice: before using an ability, consider whom it can help. Let remembrance of Lakshmanji turn strength into patient, compassionate service.
