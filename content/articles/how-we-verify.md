# How the text on Ramayana.com was checked

Ramayana.com checked its Ramcharitmanas transcription against printed Gita Press editions through two independent AI readings of every page, comparison with the page images, and checks of verse counters and totals. The work includes. This page explains the editions used, the checks performed, and the limits of that verification.

## The editions we use

The text of Goswami Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas comes from two Gita Press editions. Publication code 790 supplies the original Awadhi text and the Hindi tika [commentary and meaning] by Hanuman Prasad Poddar. We use its fifth reprint, Vikram Samvat 2073.

Publication code 1318 is the Romanised edition with English translation. We use the revised edition's sixteenth reprint, published in 2019. J.P. Agarwal prepared the revised edition. It names no single translator and records that the first consolidated English edition appeared in 1968.

These editions provide a printed source for the scripture, Hindi commentary and English translation presented together on the site. Naming the editions makes the source of each part clear.

## How the printed pages became the online text

Every printed page received two independent AI readings. Those readings were compared, and disagreements were checked against the page image. The printed page was the reference for resolving a difference.

Every verse was also matched to its printed counter. This checks that a passage occupies its intended place in the text. The counts were then reconciled against the edition's contents.

These checks address different kinds of error. Comparing readings can reveal a misread word. Matching counters can reveal a misplaced passage. Reconciling totals can reveal a gap that is less obvious while reading individual pages.

## What the numbers mean

The checked edition contains 218 topics, 1,074 dohas [couplets] and 6,157 verse units across its seven kands [books].

These are different counts. A topic is a grouping in the contents. A doha is a verse form. The total of verse units covers the text's different verse forms, so 6,157 is not a count of dohas.

Matching these totals helps establish completeness and order. It does not establish that every letter is correct. A transcription can have the expected number of passages and still contain a typing or recognition error.

## What this verification claims

The verification is AI-assisted. It is not a human editorial certification or a publisher's certification.

That distinction matters when you rely on a word, a meaning or a reference. The site presents a transcription checked against named printed editions. It does not claim that Gita Press has certified the online text, or that every passage has received a complete human editorial review.

Errors may remain. Readers can help identify them.

## How to report an error

You can report an error on any verse. Point to the word or passage that concerns you and explain the difference you have noticed. If you are comparing a printed copy, include its edition and the relevant page or verse reference.

The next step is comparison with the source page. A report identifies something to examine. The printed edition provides the basis for deciding whether the transcription needs correction.

## How AI is used elsewhere on the site

AI also assists with drafting explanatory pages. It is never used to compose the scripture or its translations. Its role in transcription is to read existing printed material.

Read slowly. If a word gives you pause, use the verse's error report to help us check it.
