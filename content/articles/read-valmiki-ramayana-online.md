## What is the Valmiki Ramayana?

The Valmiki Ramayana is the Sanskrit epic by Maharshi Valmiki. It is called the *Adi Kavya*, the first poem, and Maharshi Valmiki is honoured as the Adi Kavi. Tradition counts about 24,000 shlokas in its seven kandas. Devotees place the events it describes in the Treta Yuga.

Tradition tells that Maharshi Valmiki uttered the first shloka in grief after a hunter killed one of a pair of krauncha birds beside the Tamasa river. Lava and Kusha, the sons of Shri Ram and Mata Sita, learned the poem from him and sang it. The scripture thus comes to us as a poem to be heard as well as read.

## How the kandas and sargas are organised

A *kanda* is a major division of the epic. Each kanda contains chapters called *sargas*. The seven kandas, in order, are Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kishkindha, Sundara, Yuddha and Uttara. Yuddha Kanda is also called Lanka Kanda.

The edition on Ramayana.com contains 77 sargas in Bala, 119 in Ayodhya, 76 in Aranya, 67 in Kishkindha, 68 in Sundara, 128 in Yuddha and 113 in Uttara. These make 648 sargas altogether. Reading one sarga at a time gives us a clear place to pause and return.

### Is this the same as the Ramcharitmanas?

The Valmiki Ramayana is Maharshi Valmiki's Sanskrit epic. The Ramcharitmanas is Goswami Tulsidas's retelling in Awadhi, composed in the sixteenth century. Both tell the story of Shri Ram. In everyday speech in north India, “Ramayan” usually refers to the Ramcharitmanas, which many Hindi-speaking families read and recite.
