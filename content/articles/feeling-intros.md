## Courage

Goswami Tulsidas shows courage through Hanumanji, whose strength serves Shri Ram. When Jamvant reminds him of his strength, Hanumanji prepares to cross the sea for Mata Sita. His task gives his courage a purpose. For example, a student may hesitate to admit a mistake before the class. Yet accepting responsibility gives that student a reason to speak despite fear. Similarly, we grow courageous when we give greater importance to the duty before us than to our discomfort.

## Devotion

In the Ramcharitmanas, Goswami Tulsidas shows devotion taking the form of service. Hanumanji crosses the sea, finds Mata Sita and carries her message back to Shri Ram. His love directs his effort. For example, someone caring for an elderly parent may prepare the same meal each morning. The task repeats, yet attention keeps it from becoming mechanical. Similarly, devotion asks us to bring remembrance of Shri Ram into the duties already entrusted to us. Begin with one task and perform it with care.

## Dharma

Goswami Tulsidas teaches dharma through choices that demand sacrifice. Shri Ram accepts fourteen years of exile, while Bharat refuses the throne and seeks Him at Chitrakoot. Their conduct places duty above personal advantage. For example, a shopkeeper may discover that a customer has paid twice. Keeping the money would be easy, especially when business is slow. Returning it gives honesty precedence over gain. Similarly, we practise dharma by doing what is right when another choice would benefit us.

## Peace

Goswami Tulsidas teaches us to place trust in Shri Ram while attending to our actions. Trust quiets the demand that every result must follow our wishes. It leaves room for steady effort. For example, a gardener can water a sapling and protect its roots, but cannot make it grow by checking its height every hour. Such checking only increases restlessness. Similarly, do the work that belongs to today, then turn the mind towards Shri Ram instead of repeatedly measuring the result.

## Love

In the Ramcharitmanas, love becomes visible through care and attention. Goswami Tulsidas places Shabari's hospitality within Shri Ram's forest life, showing how an ordinary act can become an offering. For example, someone may put away a phone when a family member begins to speak. Giving full attention requires little money or preparation, yet it asks us to set aside our own preoccupation. Similarly, let love guide how we receive the person before us. Give that person the care the moment requires.

## Surrender

Goswami Tulsidas presents surrender through Vibhishan, who comes to Shri Ram and places himself under His protection. Surrender gives our dependence a clear direction. We seek the Lord's shelter and become willing to follow Him. For example, a learner struggling with a difficult passage must accept a teacher's correction before practice can improve. Asking for help while rejecting every instruction leaves the difficulty unchanged. Similarly, when we turn to Shri Ram, let us also examine which attachments prevent us from following dharma.
