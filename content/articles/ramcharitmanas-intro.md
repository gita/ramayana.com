## What the Ramcharitmanas is

'Which Ramayana have we been hearing at home since childhood?' For most Hindi-speaking families, it is the Ramcharitmanas, Goswami Tulsidas's retelling of Shri Ram's story in Awadhi, a language of the people of the Ram Janmabhoomi region. Begun in Ayodhya in 1574, it brings the story into the language of home, where we can hear it, recite it and return to it.

Think of a mother teaching her child. She chooses words the child can hold close. Familiar words open the heart. The Ramcharitmanas has that nearness. In north India, when someone says 'Ramayan', they usually mean this beloved text.

Its seven kands [books] carry the story and its teachings. Goswami Tulsidas writes mainly in chaupai [four quarter-lines] and doha [couplets], forms that lend themselves to reading aloud and recitation.

The telling itself invites us to listen alongside others. Shiva tells Parvati, Kakbhushundi tells Garuda, Yajnavalkya tells Bharadwaj, and Goswami Tulsidas tells his readers. We, too, become listeners.

What should we carry from that listening?

> राम नाम मनिदीप धरु जीह देहरीं द्वार। तुलसी भीतर बाहेरहुँ जौं चाहसि उजिआर॥
>
> (Bal Kand, doha 21)

Place the jewel-lamp of Shri Ram's Name upon the threshold of the tongue, Goswami Tulsidas teaches, if you seek light within and without. A lamp at the doorway serves both sides. Remembering Shri Ram can likewise guide our thoughts and our speech.

This reader carries the original Awadhi text alongside the Gita Press Hindi tika [commentary and meaning] by Hanuman Prasad Poddar and the Gita Press English translation.
