## Bal Kand

Shri Ram's story opens with reverence, preparing the heart to receive His deeds. In Bal Kand, Goswami Tulsidas begins with invocations and the frame stories through which the telling unfolds. The account of Shiva and Sati comes before the birth of Shri Ram and His brothers in Ayodhya, in the household of King Dashrath. Thus, the reader approaches Shri Ram through prayer before entering the events of His life.

The story then turns to the sage Vishwamitra and the episode of Ahilya. From there, it moves towards Mata Sita's swayamvar. Shri Ram breaks Shiva's bow, and the events lead to the wedding at Janakpur. What begins in invocation now takes the form of celebration, as Shri Ram and Mata Sita are united in marriage.

This kand teaches us to approach Shri Ram's story with reverence and an attentive heart. Bal Kand contains 361 dohas.

## Ayodhya Kand

Shri Ram's readiness to honour duty becomes clear when the promise of a crown gives way to exile. In Ayodhya Kand, King Dashrath decides to crown Him. At Manthara's prompting, Kaikeyi asks for her two boons, and Shri Ram must leave for the forest for fourteen years. Mata Sita and Lakshman accompany Him. The preparations for a coronation give way to departure.

Kevat ferries them across the Ganga, and the story moves from Ayodhya into the life of exile. King Dashrath dies. Bharat refuses the throne and goes to meet Shri Ram at Chitrakoot. Their meeting brings the question of kingship back before us, with Shri Ram's paduka taking their place in the account.

This kand teaches that love and duty ask us to place what is right above what we desire. Ayodhya Kand contains 326 dohas, allowing these events and their meaning to unfold at length.

## Aranya Kand

Life in the forest brings Shri Ram, Mata Sita and Lakshman into circumstances that test patience, judgement and devotion. In Aranya Kand, they live at Panchavati. The episode of Shurpanakha is followed by the appearance of the golden deer. The story then reaches the abduction of Mata Sita by Ravan, and the forest becomes the setting of separation.

Jatayu fights Ravan and dies. His part in the story places service before us through an act of resistance, even when the cost is his own life. The account also brings us to Shabari and her hospitality. Amid the sorrow of separation, this meeting gives attention to the simple act of receiving and serving Shri Ram.

This kand teaches that devotion finds expression in what we offer when the occasion for service comes. Aranya Kand contains 46 dohas. Read Shabari's hospitality with the same attention as Jatayu's fight.

## Kishkindha Kand

The search for Mata Sita brings Shri Ram into new relationships of friendship and service. In Kishkindha Kand, He meets Hanumanji and Sugriv. These meetings lead into the events surrounding the killing of Bali. The story then pauses with the monsoon at Kishkindha before turning towards the search parties and the work before them.

The movement of the kand is from meeting to action. Friendship must now express itself through service, and the search for Mata Sita gives that service a clear purpose. As the account approaches its close, Jamvant reminds Hanumanji of his strength. Attention turns to the one who will carry the search forward across the sea.

This kand teaches that strength becomes useful when we remember it and place it in the service of Shri Ram. Kishkindha Kand contains 30 dohas. Carry Jamvant's reminder into the next kand, where Hanumanji acts upon it.

## Sunderkand

Hanumanji now puts his strength into the service of Shri Ram. In Sunderkand, he leaps across the sea to Lanka, meets Vibhishan and finds Mata Sita in the Ashok Vatika. He gives her Shri Ram's ring. The search reaches its purpose in this meeting, and Hanumanji's service becomes a means of bringing word between Shri Ram and Mata Sita.

The burning of Lanka follows, and Hanumanji returns with Mata Sita's message. Yet the kand continues beyond his return. Vibhishan surrenders to Shri Ram, and Shri Ram asks the ocean for a way across. The story thus moves from one servant's crossing to the question of how the sea will be crossed for the events ahead.

This kand teaches us to give our strength a clear purpose through service and to place our trust in Shri Ram. Sunderkand contains 60 dohas. Attend to how each completed task opens the way to the next.

## Lanka Kand

The way to Lanka now opens through the building of the bridge across the sea. In Lanka Kand, the story moves into war. Kumbhkaran and Meghnad enter the account, Lakshman is wounded, and the Sanjivani becomes part of the effort to save him. These events bring the demands of service into the midst of battle.

Ravan dies, and Vibhishan is crowned. The account then turns to Mata Sita's fire ordeal. In Goswami Tulsidas's telling, this is the return of the real Mata Sita, who has been kept with Agni. Shri Ram and Mata Sita return to Ayodhya by the Pushpak, and the coronation follows. The story moves from the struggle in Lanka to the fulfilment awaiting them in Ayodhya.

This kand teaches steadfastness in duty through suffering, conflict and the responsibilities that follow victory. Lanka Kand contains 121 dohas. Give the return and coronation the same attention as the battle.

## Uttar Kand

With Shri Ram's coronation accomplished, the story turns towards Ram Rajya and the teaching of devotion. In Uttar Kand, the account of His rule leads into the dialogues of Kakbhushundi and Garuda. The reader who has followed the events is now asked to attend closely to what they teach about a life centred on Shri Ram.

Bhakti becomes the focus of the telling. Through these dialogues, Goswami Tulsidas brings the heart back to devotion and to the praise of the Name as the remedy for the Kali Yuga. The closing movement gives the reader a practice to carry into daily life. Hearing Shri Ram's deeds should lead us to remember Him.
