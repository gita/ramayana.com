## Bal Kand

Bal Kand opens with invocations and the telling of how Shri Ram’s story comes to be heard. The frame stories prepare us to receive it with reverence. The account of Shiva and Sati comes before the birth of Shri Ram and His brothers in Ayodhya, in the household of King Dashrath. From these beginnings, the narrative moves to the sage Vishwamitra and the episode of Ahilya.

At Mata Sita’s swayamvar, Shri Ram breaks Shiva’s bow. The story then leads to the wedding at Janakpur. What begins in prayer thus unfolds into the events through which we come to know Shri Ram, first in Ayodhya and then through the episodes that lead to His marriage with Mata Sita.

The teaching is to approach Shri Ram’s story with reverence, allowing attention to His deeds to nourish devotion. Bal Kand contains 361 dohas.

## Ayodhya Kand

King Dashrath decides to crown Shri Ram, and Ayodhya Kand brings us to the moment when the throne appears ready for Him. Then Manthara prompts Kaikeyi to claim her two boons. The course of events changes. Shri Ram goes into exile for fourteen years, accompanied by Mata Sita and Lakshman. The story moves from the prospect of coronation to life away from Ayodhya.

Kevat ferries them across the Ganga. King Dashrath dies, and Bharat refuses the throne. His meeting with Shri Ram at Chitrakoot brings the brothers together, yet it does not lead to Shri Ram’s return. The paduka, Shri Ram’s sandals, take their place in the account of Bharat’s devotion.

This kand teaches that dharma asks us to remain faithful when circumstances overturn our hopes. Shri Ram’s exile and Bharat’s refusal of the throne place duty above personal gain. Ayodhya Kand contains 326 dohas.

## Aranya Kand

Aranya Kand follows Shri Ram, Mata Sita and Lakshman into their forest life at Panchavati. The account moves through the episode of Shurpanakha to the appearance of the golden deer. Then comes the abduction of Mata Sita by Ravan. The forest narrative now centres on separation, and the events reveal how each person acts when faced with suffering and wrongdoing.

Jatayu fights Ravan and dies. His place in the story rests upon the service he offers in that moment of danger. Later, Shabari receives Shri Ram with hospitality. The kand thus brings together two forms of service: the struggle to protect Mata Sita and the welcome offered to Shri Ram.

The teaching is that devotion finds expression in action according to the need before us. Jatayu serves through his fight, while Shabari serves through her hospitality. Aranya Kand contains 46 dohas. Read these episodes with attention to the service each one offers.

## Kishkindha Kand

Kishkindha Kand brings Shri Ram into the company of Hanumanji and Sugriv. These meetings turn the story towards the work of finding Mata Sita. The narrative proceeds through the killing of Bali and the monsoon at Kishkindha. The search then takes organised form as parties set out to find her. The purpose before them is clear, and the account follows their efforts towards its fulfilment.

At the close of the kand, Jamvant reminds Hanumanji of his strength. This reminder prepares the way for the deeds that follow in Sunderkand. Before Hanumanji crosses the sea, he is called to recognise the strength with which he can serve.

The teaching is to remember our capacity for service and put it to use when duty calls. Jamvant’s reminder directs Hanumanji’s attention towards the task before him. Kishkindha Kand contains 30 dohas. Carry that connection between strength and service into the next kand.

## Sunderkand

In Sunderkand, Hanumanji leaps across the sea to Lanka in search of Mata Sita. He meets Vibhishan, then finds her in the Ashok Vatika and gives her Shri Ram’s ring. The search reaches its purpose in this meeting. The narrative continues through the burning of Lanka and Hanumanji’s return with Mata Sita’s message. His deeds remain directed towards the service entrusted to him.

The kand then moves from Hanumanji’s return to Vibhishan’s surrender to Shri Ram. As the story prepares for the crossing to Lanka, Shri Ram asks the ocean for a way across. Thus, the account carries us from one servant’s leap over the sea to the need for a passage across it.

The teaching is to join strength with devotion, keeping the Lord’s service before us in every action. Sunderkand contains 60 dohas. Read Hanumanji’s deeds with attention to the purpose that guides them.

## Lanka Kand

Lanka Kand opens with the bridge across the sea, making the passage to Lanka possible. War follows. The account moves through Kumbhkaran and Meghnad, then Lakshman’s wound and the Sanjivani. These episodes lead towards the death of Ravan. After the battle, Vibhishan is crowned, and the narrative turns from warfare to the events that prepare for the return to Ayodhya.

Mata Sita’s fire ordeal appears here in Goswami Tulsidas’s telling as the return of the real Mata Sita, who has been kept with Agni. Shri Ram then returns to Ayodhya by the Pushpak. The coronation follows, bringing the story from the crossing of the sea and the struggle in Lanka to Shri Ram’s rule in Ayodhya.

The teaching is that strength must serve dharma, with rightful rule as its purpose. Lanka Kand contains 121 dohas. Keep this purpose in view as the account passes from battle to coronation.

## Uttar Kand

Uttar Kand brings us into Ram Rajya, then turns towards the dialogues of Kakbhushundi and Garuda. After the coronation, the story gives space to the teaching of bhakti. The events we have followed now lead us to consider how devotion is to take root in our own lives. Listening remains an active part of this learning, as the dialogues carry the teaching forward.

The kand praises the remedy available in the Kali Yuga: the Name. Here, attention rests upon the practice of remembering Shri Ram. The account of His deeds leads naturally to devotion to Him, and the teaching directs us towards a practice we can continue each day.
