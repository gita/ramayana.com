## Bal Kand

How do we begin reading about Shri Ram when the heart is eager to reach his birth? Bal Kand first invites us to listen. Through invocations and the frame stories, Goswami Tulsidas prepares the heart to receive what follows. We hear of Shiva and Sati before entering Ayodhya, where Shri Ram and his brothers are born to King Dashrath.

Then the story opens out. The sage Vishwamitra enters, and we come to the episode of Ahilya. At Mata Sita's swayamvar [ceremony for choosing a husband], Shri Ram breaks Shiva's bow. The wedding takes us to Janakpur. What began in prayer has brought us into the happiness of a household.

There is a gentle lesson here. Before we hurry towards what we want to see, we can learn to listen with care. Even a beloved story deserves our fresh attention.

Bal Kand contains 361 dohas [couplets]. Begin with a little reading, and give it your whole attention.

## Ayodhya Kand

A household can be preparing for celebration when an unexpected demand changes everything. Ayodhya Kand brings us into that sudden turn. King Dashrath decides to crown Shri Ram. At Manthara's prompting, Kaikeyi asks for her two boons, and the story moves from preparations for a coronation to fourteen years of exile.

Mata Sita and Lakshman accompany Shri Ram. Kevat ferries them across the Ganga. Back in Ayodhya, Dashrath dies, and Bharat refuses the throne. His meeting with Shri Ram at Chitrakoot brings the love between the brothers before us. The paduka [sandals] become part of this account of love and responsibility.

We know how quickly disappointment can make us think only of ourselves. Here, amid loss and separation, we are asked to look closely at what love requires of us.

Ayodhya Kand contains 326 dohas. When a plan changes today, pause before speaking. Let your first response make room for someone else's need.

## Aranya Kand

How do we hold on to tenderness when life becomes painful? Aranya Kand takes us into forest life at Panchavati, then into events that bring separation and grief. The episode of Shurpanakha is followed by the golden deer. Ravan abducts Mata Sita, and the forest account becomes a search marked by her absence.

Jatayu fights and dies. His part in the story asks us to pause. We can be so eager to know what happens next that we hurry past an act of courage. Then comes Shabari's hospitality, bringing the simple act of welcoming a guest into this account of loss.

A home need not be grand for welcome to matter. A person need not be certain of success before offering help.

Aranya Kand contains 46 dohas. It teaches us to keep courage and kindness alive in difficulty. Offer someone a little care today, without waiting for your own troubles to end.

## Kishkindha Kand

Sometimes we need someone else to remind us of the strength we have forgotten. Kishkindha Kand brings that tender moment through Jamvant and Hanumanji. Before it, the story gathers new companions around Shri Ram through his meeting with Hanumanji and Sugriv.

The killing of Bali follows. Then the monsoon comes at Kishkindha, and the account moves towards the sending of search parties. The search gives the story a clear task, one that calls for effort beyond a single person's reach. When Jamvant reminds Hanumanji of his strength, encouragement itself becomes an act of help.

Think of a classroom where a hesitant child can do the work but needs a teacher's confidence. We too can help another person recognise what they can offer.

Kishkindha Kand contains 30 dohas. Its lesson is to receive encouragement humbly and give it generously. Today, remind someone of a strength they can put to good use.

## Sunderkand

What does devotion look like when there is work to be done? In Sunderkand, Hanumanji leaps across the sea to Lanka. He meets Vibhishan and finds Mata Sita in the Ashok Vatika. He gives her Shri Ram's ring, bringing the story to a moment where a small object carries the weight of a message.

The burning of Lanka follows. Hanumanji returns with Mata Sita's message, and the account then brings Vibhishan to surrender before Shri Ram. At the sea, Shri Ram asks the ocean for a way across. Each event turns our attention towards the task still ahead.

Like a family member entrusted with an urgent errand, Hanumanji shows us devotion expressed through service. Love gives our work its purpose.

Sunderkand contains 60 dohas. Its teaching asks us to make our care useful. Choose one responsibility today and attend to it with your heart fully present.

## Lanka Kand

The search for Mata Sita has brought Shri Ram to the sea. Lanka Kand now carries the story across it, with the building of the bridge and the war that follows. Kumbhkaran and Meghnad enter the account. Lakshman's wound and the Sanjivani bring another urgent turn before the death of Ravan.

Vibhishan is crowned. In Goswami Tulsidas's telling, Mata Sita's fire ordeal is the return of the real Sita, who had been kept with Agni. Then comes the return to Ayodhya by the Pushpak and Shri Ram's coronation.

After so much conflict, the return draws our attention towards home. We can understand something of that longing whenever a difficult responsibility keeps us away from those we love.

Lanka Kand contains 121 dohas. Its events ask us to consider what our efforts are meant to serve. Before beginning today's demanding task, remember the people whose welfare gives that work meaning.

## Uttar Kand

What do we give our attention to when the great events have passed? Uttar Kand brings us into Ram Rajya [the rule of Shri Ram], then towards the dialogues of Kakbhushundi and Garuda. The story makes room for listening, questions and teaching. We are invited to stay and hear.

The teaching on bhakti [devotion] comes before us, together with praise of the Name as the remedy in Kali Yuga [the age named in this teaching]. After following so many events, we are asked to attend to the heart that has been listening.

It is rather like returning from a gathering where a loving word stays with you. The gathering ends. The word can still shape how you speak at home.

Uttar Kand contains 130 dohas. Its teaching calls us to carry devotion into ordinary hours. Keep Shri Ram's Name close today, and let it bring gentleness into your next conversation.
