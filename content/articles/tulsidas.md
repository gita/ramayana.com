# Goswami Tulsidas: his life, his works and the Ramcharitmanas

Goswami Tulsidas was the devotee and poet who composed Shri Ramcharitmanas, the Awadhi retelling of Shri Ram's story that Hindi-speaking families read and recite at home. He lived in the sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries. Like a mother bringing a lesson into her child's own language, he gave readers words they could understand, sing and carry into daily life.

## His life in brief

Goswami Tulsidas lived roughly from 1532 to 1623 CE, although some traditions give 1511 as his birth year. Most accounts place his birth in the Rajapur region. He spent his later life in Varanasi and left his body at Assi Ghat.

These details give us a brief outline. His works invite a closer acquaintance. We meet a devotee teaching us to turn towards Shri Ram, to examine our conduct and to make room for devotion in ordinary life.

Begin with his words.

## The night the Manas began

Goswami Tulsidas records in Bal Kand that he began the Ramcharitmanas in Ayodhya on Ram Navami, Tuesday, in Vikram Samvat 1631, or 1574 CE.

He chose Awadhi, the language of the people of the Ram Janmabhoomi region. The title Ramcharitmanas means “the Manas lake of Shri Ram's deeds”. A lake invites us to sit beside it and return. So does this scripture.

Its seven kands [major divisions] hold Shri Ram's story within conversations. Shiva speaks to Parvati, Kakbhushundi to Garuda, Yajnavalkya to Bharadwaj, and Goswami Tulsidas to his readers. We enter as listeners.

## His other works

His devotion found many forms. Some works tell a story, some offer prayer, and others ask us to look honestly at ourselves.

- **Hanuman Chalisa:** Forty verses praising Hanumanji, the most recited Hindi prayer.
- **Vinay Patrika:** A “petition” of hymns addressed to Shri Ram, written in Braj Bhasha.
- **Kavitavali:** Shri Ram's story in kavitta and savaiya metres.
- **Dohavali:** A collection of dohas [couplets] on dharma [right conduct] and devotion.
- **Gitavali:** Shri Ram's story told through songs in Braj Bhasha.
- **Janaki Mangal:** A work celebrating the wedding of Mata Sita and Shri Ram.
- **Parvati Mangal:** A work celebrating the wedding of Parvati and Shiva.
- **Barvai Ramayan:** A short Ramayana composed in the barvai metre.
- **Ramlala Nahachhu:** A folk-style song about the child Shri Ram's nail-paring ceremony.
- **Vairagya Sandipani:** Verses on dispassion and the nature of a saint.
- **Krishna Gitavali:** Songs of Shri Krishna.

You need not read everything at once. Choose a work that draws your attention, then give it the patience you would give a person speaking from the heart.

## Tulsidas and Hanumanji

Goswami Tulsidas is regarded as a devotee to whom Hanumanji granted the darshan [sacred sight] of Shri Ram. His reverence for Hanumanji also speaks directly through the Ramcharitmanas:

> प्रनवउँ पवनकुमार खल बन पावक ग्यान घन। जासु हृदय आगार बसहिं राम सर चाप धर॥
>
> Bal Kand, soratha 17.

He bows to Hanumanji as a fire to the forest of wickedness and an embodiment of knowledge, whose heart houses Shri Ram bearing bow and arrows.

Think of a room prepared for a beloved guest. What we keep there matters. This praise invites us to consider what occupies our own hearts. Can we make more room for remembrance and service?

## Why his Ramayana is sung in homes

A lesson can remain in a notebook. A song follows us into the kitchen.

The Ramcharitmanas is written mainly in chaupai [four quarter-lines] and doha, alongside sorathas and chhands [verse forms]. Its form gives families words to recite together. It is the Ramayana most Hindi-speaking families read and recite, and in everyday speech across north India, “Ramayan” usually means the Ramcharitmanas.

Goswami Tulsidas gives us a homely image for remembrance:

> राम नाम मनिदीप धरु जीह देहरीं द्वार। तुलसी भीतर बाहेरहुँ जौं चाहसि उजिआर॥
>
> Bal Kand, doha 21.

Place the jewel-lamp of Shri Ram's Name at the doorway of the tongue, he teaches, if you wish for light within and without.

The tongue is a doorway we use all day. Let remembrance help us choose what passes through it.

## How to read him today

Open the [Ramcharitmanas](/ramcharitmanas) and read a small passage with its Hindi commentary or English translation. Read the original aloud if you can. Pause over one teaching before moving on.

Read Goswami Tulsidas alongside Maharshi Valmiki, whose Sanskrit Ramayana is called the Adi Kavya [the first poem]. Let your attention deepen through both tellings.

For a shorter daily reading, visit the [Hanuman Chalisa](https://hanumanchalisa.net). Keep the practice manageable. After reading, choose one action through which the words can enter your day.

## Questions people ask

### When and where was Tulsidas born?

Goswami Tulsidas is generally dated to roughly 1532 to 1623 CE, though some traditions give 1511 as his birth year. Most accounts place his birth in the Rajapur region. He spent his later life in Varanasi, where he left his body at Assi Ghat.

### Did Tulsidas write the original Ramayana?

Maharshi Valmiki composed the Sanskrit Ramayana, known as the Adi Kavya, or first poem. Goswami Tulsidas composed the Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi in the sixteenth century. Both tell Shri Ram's story. Reading them alongside each other allows us to attend carefully to each telling with reverence.

### When did Tulsidas begin the Ramcharitmanas?

Goswami Tulsidas records in Bal Kand that he began the Ramcharitmanas in Ayodhya on Ram Navami, Tuesday, in Vikram Samvat 1631, corresponding to 1574 CE. He composed it in Awadhi, the language of the people of the Ram Janmabhoomi region, giving families a scripture to read and recite.

### Should I begin with the Ramcharitmanas or the Hanuman Chalisa?

You can begin with either. The Hanuman Chalisa offers forty verses praising Hanumanji, while the Ramcharitmanas opens Shri Ram's story across seven kands. Choose a reading you can return to regularly, with attention to its meaning. Today, read a little, remember Shri Ram, and let your next words be kind.
